Research Overview ​

Design and develop flexible sensors to enhance the robot's perception and physical interaction capabilities, and integrate embodied intelligence models to promote more comfortable and efficient human-machine interfaces.💫



Research highlights ​

1、Toward Human-Level Tactile Intelligence: A Large-Scale, High-Density Iontronic Skin ​

This project develops large-scale, high-density iontronic skins with a neural-inspired hierarchical processing architecture for human-level tactile intelligence. The system integrates over 10,000 sensing units, achieving a spatial density of up to 1,110 units cm⁻² and readout rates of up to 100 FPS through frequency-encoded parallel acquisition and crosstalk suppression. By combining hierarchical data compression with deep learning, it enables efficient tactile information processing. Integrated into robotic hands and the torso, the system supports fine texture sensing, dexterous manipulation, and pain-inspired self-protection, advancing whole-body tactile perception and safe human–robot interaction.

Zhibin Li*, Binxu Da*, Junli Shi*, et al. Chenlong Fu✉, Taihong Wang✉, Chuan Fei Guo✉ Large-scale iontronic skins with neural-hierarchical processing architecture. Nature Machine Intelligence (2026).

2、Bimodal Iontronic Skins for Large-Area Robotic Coverage ​

Here, we develop a flexible bimodal skin with modular design for large-area coverage, integrating pressure and temperature sensing to enable intent-driven collaboration. We implement full-coverage skins on entire robotic arm, integrating over 768 pressure and 75 temperature sensing units, and develop a rapid, crosstalk-free readout interface based on a frequency-coding architecture. Our system allows subconscious touches at any location on the skin to be accurately interpreted in real time, enabling precise determination of human intent. We demonstrate that our technique facilitates smooth, adaptive interaction for individuals with mobility impairments, even under complex or emergency conditions. The human-centered interaction enabled by the large-area coverage, bimodal skin may be extended to many other applications.

Zhibin Li*, Junli Shi*, Xinxin Chen*, et al. , Chenlong Fu✉, Taihong Wang✉, Chuan Fei Guo✉, Bimodal iontronic skins powered by edge intelligence for real-time collaborative interaction. National Science Review, 2026, nwag111
